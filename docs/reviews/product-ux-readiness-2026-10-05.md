# BRAVOS product and UX readiness — 5 October 2026

## Readiness verdict

BRAVOS has substantial implemented business workflows and a coherent storefront identity, but it is not yet an accepted customer or staff release. The useful next milestone is a populated, representative acceptance pass on the actual devices used by shoppers and branch staff. Engineering passes establish bounded correctness; they do not establish that cashiers can work quickly, customers can choose merchandise confidently, or operators can recover a hosted service.

The current review found concrete friction in POS discovery, storefront discovery and checkout recovery, plus mobile product presentation that needs representative acceptance. A critical local-preview client initialization blocker has been repaired and actual-product basket/quote interactions now pass in live local Chromium. Corrections to other UX issues remain **pending verification** until current-source evidence is recorded below. The sparse real catalog and inconsistent variation photography also limit what visual review can establish.

The subjective maturity percentages in [the 4 October delivery assessment](../planning/project-delivery-status-2026-10-04.md) are historical planning judgments, not product acceptance. This report adds no completion percentage and does not replace that historical report.

## Basis and limits

The requirements baseline is [bravos.md](../../bravos.md), refined by [the Phase 1 operational updates](../planning/phase-1-operational-updates.md). Arabic is primary; desktop/tablet POS must support simple staff work, and storefront and management must work well on phones. The later branch-stock, inventory-first intake, tender allocation and online recognition rules take precedence over earlier central-stock/cash-only wording.

This report combines the coordinating agent's current product audit with the bounded prior evidence in [4 October storefront verification](storefront-completion-2026-10-04.md), [2 October reliability review](storefront-sf12-reliability-review-2026-10-02.md) and [2 October visual follow-up](storefront-visual-followup-2026-10-02.md). Prior test results remain dated evidence. The current audit uses a small real catalog with two products; it cannot establish a convincing larger collection or final merchandising quality. This report does not claim a fresh full-system audit of every internal module, physical-device acceptance, production measurements or hosted operation.

Priority meanings: **Critical (P0)** blocks the affected transaction; **High (P1)** should close before the corresponding staff/customer acceptance; **Medium (P2)** needs review before final acceptance; **Polish** covers nonblocking visual refinement. A remaining release gate is not automatically a demonstrated application defect.

## Implemented foundation versus accepted experience

The implemented foundation includes separate branch balances, inventory-first intake, existing variant-pair merchandising, physical-sale attribution, cash/manual online/split tender, durable offline sale handling, readable references, transaction/phone return lookup, confirmation-based online recognition and reversal workflows. Prior Phase 1 and system-improvement reviews record their bounded engineering verification. This pass does not relabel those results as fresh acceptance of every staff screen.

The storefront already implements catalog search/category/sort, variant availability, gallery/cart, COD quote/submission and retry handling. The recent reliability review includes a real isolated shopping journey and confirms that a pending request does not change stock or completed-sale analytics. The 4 October review records a mobile keyboard obstruction repair. These are useful implementation and regression facts, while populated customer acceptance remains open.

Business meaning must remain explicit in both interfaces and acceptance scripts:

- A pending online request holds no stock and contributes no recognized sales value. Confirmation selects one branch able to fulfill the entire basket, deducts its stock and recognizes value once; shipment/delivery do not repeat those effects.
- Cancellation/failed delivery reverses the recognized value according to the recorded workflow. Protected merchandise becomes available only after physical receipt and inspection of sellable quantities.
- POS browse and cached quantities must stay scoped to the active authorized branch. A completed physical sale remains durable through an outage and must synchronize once with its original attribution and payment facts.
- Disconnected tills cannot promise an instantly consistent global stock balance. Acceptance must cover later reconciliation and honest stock/sync feedback without discarding completed sales.
- Return inspection and sellable restoration use the receiving branch while retaining original costs and sale attribution. Sensitive costs/profit remain absent from public and ordinary employee responses/caches.

## Bounded completion plan

1. **Finish and verify the current usability changes.** Freeze the POS and storefront diff, review behavior against the superseding rules, and complete the broad checks on that exact source. Inspect populated desktop/tablet POS and narrow-screen storefront captures with live interactions. Retain the now-passing realistic remote same-staff account-transition regression and immutable queued-sale assertions in the final gate. Record failures and repairs honestly. Exit: the changed paths have passing evidence, remaining findings have a disposition, and the release candidate is identifiable.

2. **Complete populated acceptance on actual devices.** Use approved branch data, permissions, delivery configuration and a representative assortment. Cashiers should locate a product without memorizing its code, select an actual variant, edit a cart, complete each tender type and recover an offline sale. Management should exercise receiving, merchandising, order confirmation/cancellation/inspection, Returns, expenses and reports using representative cases. Shoppers should find an item, compare variations, understand availability, repair a cart and submit a pending request on physical iOS Safari/Android Chrome. Include mobile keyboards, safe areas, scrolling, focus and real tablet POS interaction. Exit: owner/staff/customer representative acceptance with recorded blockers resolved; no simulated-device claim presented as physical evidence.

3. **Accept final merchandise presentation and brand.** Review the current dark identity, cover composition, logos, Arabic font roles, motion and gallery with consistent basic product photography and factual product information. Assess mobile selection/action hierarchy and variation framing through that review while retaining the intentional image-led direction. Studio campaigns are optional; a usable limited-asset design is required. Exit: owner approves brand/content and the populated responsive presentation.

4. **Close production performance and accessibility gates.** Use the established measurement procedure on an authorized running production build with representative media. Close SF-11's matched baseline/final measurement and interaction evidence, and the remaining SF-12 integrated accessibility/device checks. Report lab results with their scope; do not label them field performance. Historical rejected preview launches are not runtime evidence. Exit: documented current production results and accepted limits.

5. **Prepare release and operator recovery after website completion.** Hosting/domain booking remains deferred until the approved completion point. Then verify hosted HTTPS/proxy/services, database and media persistence, monitoring, backup/restore and owner recovery. Business-data backups do not restore missing image binaries; preserve images separately. Train operators on active branch, tender, pending/confirmed orders, unsynchronized counts, retries and recovery exceptions. Pilot internal operations first, reconcile opening stock/totals, then enable public ordering after customer-flow and delivery acceptance. Exit: approved staged launch, successful recovery drill and named handover responsibilities.

No dates, elapsed-time promises or new business policies are implied by this plan. Final release approval depends on the recorded acceptance gates rather than a completion percentage.
